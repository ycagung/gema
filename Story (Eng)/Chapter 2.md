# Chapter 2 - Disorientation

---

**Gema**
---
"Gem, wake up. School time." said a very familiar yet distant voice. A voice which eternity has passed since I last heard of it.

"Gem, come on. It's Monday, you'll be late." 

Yep, that's right, I can confirm that it's my mother's. My body reacts slower than my mind so it's just jerking up a whole second after the realization. I can see surprise in my mother's face, maybe because I'm surprised too. Then a drop of tears fall out the corner of my eye. Not a single word can escape my mouth.

"Gem, what happened? Why are you crying?" asked she, worried.

"I-I'm... I... was just having a very bad nightmare, mom."

"Crying from a nightmare? Really? Come on. You're thirteen now. Quick, go take a shower and prepare for school."

Her voice is blurred by the storm of emotion currently in my head, heart? I don't know. I just force a smile and nod. She taps my shoulder and goes away to the kitchen.

Slowly, very slowly, I can hear my heartbeat. Raising by the seconds. Then my ears ring like I'm being blasted by a very bad microphone feedback, directly in my eardrums. And like those aren't enough, now my chest feels like it's being crushed by a car. I'm suffocation from nothing at all. My neck is burning. Tremors in my hands, trying to reach for my neck, looking for anything that is choking me. But alas, I find nothing. 

Then the second realization hit me like a truck, almost literally. I was just dead! Hit by a truck so hard my head fell off my body. Really dead! 

Then what is this? Am I in hell? Is this what hell feels like? Because I certainly am not going to heaven. But there's no way that my mother is in hell. THEN WHAT IN THE WORLD IS THIS?!

*Alright, Gem. Calm down...*

*But, my mother...*

*Analyze this first, don't panic. If you really was just decapitated, then the trauma must be suffocating to say the least.*

Slowly, I take control. Slowing down my breaths. Letting my trembling hands loose. My pounding heart begins to subside and the choking starts to go away. Leaving me drenched in my adrenaline-filled cold sweat.

I left my bed and walk out of my room. I take all visual information that I can. The same door, same desk, same walls, same floor. It really is my old house. The smell of my mother's cooking fills my nose. I bet hell doesn't smell this good and spicy. No other choice, I walk over to the kitchen.

"Mom, what date is it?" I asked her and immediately surprised by my own voice. It's so... teen-like. Then I see my own face in the mirror and gasped.

"It's June 29<sup>th</sup>, Gem. Why?" She answered, which I don't hear. I'm busy looking at the mirror. Feeling my skin, my cheeks, forehead, hair. I really look like I was in my teenage years. "Gem, what are you doing? Popping your pimples?" She asked again, which now surprised me.

"Ah, r-right. I got an annoying pimple."

Then my eyes catch a glimpse of the calendar in the corner. It's written there, year... 2010?!

"Mom, what date did you say today is?"

"June 29<sup>th</sup>, Gem. Didn't you hear me?" Her answer just makes me more anxious.

"June 29<sup>th</sup>... 2010?"

"Yes, it's 2010- What is wrong with you? You look like someone who has lost his memories."

My mind snaps back in. "I-I am fine. Maybe just overslept, my head feels like it's confusing itself. But I really am OK."

"Just go wash yourself, eat your breakfast and go to school. I don't want you to be late."

Those words slap me so hard. *Shit, school.*

---

"Gem, have you done your math homework?" asked Jonas, bringing me back to earth. I look at the clock, still 11:30, the break time right before math. Great, just great. Yesterday I was negotiating five figure contract so hard like a Wolf Street guy, now what am I going to do? Learning basic algebra? 

"Looks like I haven't," I said to Jonas. "Yeah, let's see." I open my bag and look for the math workbook, and there it is, blank answer. "Yep, I haven't," I said again to Jonas with a grin.

"Oh, no, me too. What are we going to do? The class is in ten minutes. Forget about doing the homework, I don't even understand it," he said, panicking. What a parasite.

Jonas is my close friend, or was, or is? It's hurting my head which is past and which is present. The point is that he's a fine guy to play with, or at least he was when I was still a teenager. Now he just looks like one of my useless team member who couldn't do anything beside panicking.

Without saying a word, I start to write down the answer. Really, it's just a basic algebra. I can do this in three languages while half asleep. It hasn't been thirty seconds when I put down my pen, all question has been answered just like that.

"You want to copy my homework? Fine. But this is the last time," I said flatly to Jonas, whose jaw is slacking so low I thought it fell down. Then he nods enthusiastically, picks out my book and starts to copy it letter by letter. 

I left him while he's doing his 'homework'. I'm hungry.

---

Bakso is five thousand rupiah, a rice box is four thousand, a bag of crackers is two thousand. I'm mumbling the contents of the menu like saying an inappropriate prayer. Then my hand reaches into my pocket and pulls out four thousand rupiah. Really? Now I have to starve at school too? 

"I'll just have the cracker, please," I said to the clerk, handing out two thousand. Then the school bell rings. Come on, I haven't eaten yet. 

Quickly opening the pack, I cramp a few of those into my mouth while dragging my feet back to the class. Right before the stairs, my eyes catch a view of the student council members gathering at the basketball court. Looks like they're preparing a school exhibition. A small voice in my mind whispers, why don't I propose myself as a council member? I'll be getting a lot of free time, which I can use to sleep instead of listening to boring classes. Then another voice replied, nope, too much responsibilities. 

I just started to climb the stairs when I see a pair of familiar shoes from a distant time. It didn't occur to me that doing junior high school all over again means that I'll be going through the same experience and meeting the very same person I met in my previous life. Jonas doesn't count, people like him are everywhere. But this one person, definitely counts. Looking up, I see the same face I've seen decades ago going down the stairs.

"Hey, Gem," she says. While tucking a lock of her hair behind her ear.

"Anya..." I mumble like a starstruck person. Anya is still as beautiful as I remember, still as graceful as a white swan, still as fragrant as her favorite rose back then. My body is stiffening up, I'm completely frozen like a board. Not because I'm scared or nervous. I'm fighting the urge to run and embrace her in my arms. All the while not realizing that she's right in front of me now.

"Are you okay, Gem? You're trembling. Are you sick?" she hits me with a barrage of question. Her face looks worried. When she puts the back of her hand to my forehead, I flinched. That cold and soft hand feels like a glowing hot steel plate to my skin. In a complete reflex I grab her wrist and push it off me.

"I'm... fine. Really. Those guys look like they've been waiting for you," I said while pointing my thumb to the basketball court. "Bye, Anya," I quickly walk past her up the stairs

Damn it! Why?! Why Anya?! It's not enough that I still don't understand what's going on. Why did I wake up in this body? In this year? Now I have to redo the Anya part in my life all over again?! I have to see her? Every damn day?!

I really don't want to go through this again. Knowing full well that in a few months, Anya will be the center of my universe. A very convoluted, over-complicated, and hurting universe.

---

"Gema, do question number nineteen."

That call just pulled me back to earth with double the gravity, at least it snaps me back from my daydreaming. Ms. Karin is one of the teacher that always knows if her students are listening or not. Thanks for pulling me out of this Anya rabbit hole. 

I look at that question number nineteen she spoke about. Ah, just a basic quadratic formula.

$x^2 + 10x - 24 = 0$

"Factor it into $(x - 2)$ and $(x + 12)$. And we'll get $x = 2$ and $x = -12$," I said without a hitch. Like I said, I can do this half asleep in three languages. 

She's taken aback. I was always a good math student, even in my previous life. That's why I enjoy doing programming after I became an adult. But right now, answering that question without even a slightest sign of thinking, without writing on any paper, without even an explanation of how the concept works, kind of surprised her.

"Uhm... Gema is right. So kids, that's how you..." she continued her teaching. 

And right at that time, a crucial fact that I should be getting earlier since this morning just hit me. Hard.

I came from the future! This small teenage body won't help, but my mind will. I know what will happen in my future, heck, in the future of the world even. No one is there to stop me from using this as long as I'm careful. This changes things, like... Damn! Bitcoin! Apple! Machine Learning! God, I can be rich!

Oh, shit. I said in my head, deadpan like I was just hit by a truck. But it didn't kill me this time. This time I'll do better.
